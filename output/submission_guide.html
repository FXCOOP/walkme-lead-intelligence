1. Executive Summary

This system converts raw inbound lead signals into prioritized, confidence-aware routing decisions. It is not a scoring tool — it is a decision engine that tells you how much to trust each score.

Core Principle

A lead scoring system that can't say "I'm not sure" is lying. This system surfaces uncertainty at every layer — from data confidence scoring to AI adjudication to explicit human-review flags.

What It Does

  1. Ingests leads from Google Sheets or CSV
  2. Scores each lead across 4 dimensions (ICP Fit, Intent, Engagement, Data Confidence)
  3. Reasons using GPT-4o-mini to validate or challenge each tier assignment
  4. Adjudicates conflicting signals (63% of leads triggered adjudication)
  5. Researches Tier A accounts using web search (grounded, refuses to fabricate)
  6. Routes each lead to the right team with SLAs and automated actions
  7. Evaluates itself — surfaces where it breaks, not just where it works

3. Scoring Model

Four Scoring Dimensions

A single composite score destroys information. These four dimensions preserve the signal structure that routing decisions depend on.

DimensionWeightWhat It Measures
ICP Fit40%Company size (sigmoid), industry, title seniority, geography, email legitimacy, tech stack
Intent35%Page visits (diminishing returns), form submissions, demo requests, time on site
Engagement15%Page diversity, lead source quality, content consumption, webinar attendance
Data Confidence10%Field completeness, corporate email, company verifiability, red flags, signal contradictions

Key Design Decisions

  • Sigmoid for company size — a 999-person company shouldn't score 10pts less than a 1001-person company. Formula: 30 / (1 + exp(-0.005 * (size - 500)))
  • Diminishing returns on pages — 1st high-intent page = 10pts, 2nd = 8, 3rd = 6, 4th+ = 4. Visiting 5 pages shouldn't outscore submitting a demo form.
  • Signal contradictions — high ICP + zero intent flags as "possible data import." High intent + low ICP flags as "tire-kicker risk."
  • Post-sale title detection — "Customer Success" and "Support" titles get -5pt modifier (not buyers).
  • Champion path — mid-level titles at 50K+ companies get +5pt bonus (internal champions drive enterprise deals).

4. AI Layer Design

The AI layer performs three distinct functions. Each has a specific trigger, a different purpose, and a structured JSON output schema.

FunctionTriggerModelPurpose
Lead ReasoningEvery leadgpt-4o-miniValidate tier, explain risks, recommend action, state confidence
AdjudicationFlagged leadsgpt-4o-miniResolve signal conflicts: promote / downgrade / keep / escalate
Account ResearchTier A onlygpt-4o-mini + web searchGrounded company brief with verified facts

Prompt Design Principles

  • Structured JSON output — every prompt specifies an exact schema. No free-text parsing.
  • Confidence at every layer — every response includes high/medium/low confidence. Low confidence triggers human review.
  • Grounding over generation — research refuses to fabricate. "Insufficient data" is a valid output.
  • Complementary, not redundant — AI validates the deterministic score, it doesn't re-score from scratch.

Adjudication Triggers

Leads are flagged for AI adjudication when:

  1. Final score falls in the gray zone (55-75)
  2. Data confidence is below 60
  3. Gap between highest and lowest dimension scores exceeds 30 points
  4. Signal contradictions detected (high ICP + zero intent, etc.)

In our sample run, 19 of 30 leads (63%) triggered adjudication. This is realistic — in production lead data, ambiguity is the norm.

  • Unverifiable identities — personal email + senior title claims
  • Insufficient research data — when account research couldn't verify the company

7. Dataset Design

The dataset was built to stress-test the system, not to validate it. 30 leads with deliberate ambiguity:

CategoryCountPurpose
Strong ICP matches8Confirm the engine correctly fast-tracks enterprise buyers
Partial matches7Stress-test scoring when some dimensions are strong, others absent
Clear low-fit5Verify the engine filters noise
Genuinely ambiguous10The real test — leads where the right answer depends on judgment

Realistic Messiness

The dataset includes missing fields, inconsistent formatting, and CRM-style shorthand notes — because production data looks like this:

  • Empty company, industry, or country fields on 7 leads
  • ~2500 as company size (string, not number)
  • united states vs US vs United States
  • Notes like "hotmail addr - verify?" and "ref'd by existing customer (confirmed)"

9. Production Monitoring

Two measurable signals (as requested) plus additional operational metrics:

Signal 1: Tier A to Meeting Conversion Rate

What: Of all Tier A leads, what % had a meeting booked within 14 days?

Threshold: Below 30% for any 2-week window means the scoring is too generous.

Action: Audit recent Tier A leads. Tighten ICP thresholds or increase confidence requirements.

Signal 2: Manual Override Frequency

What: How often do AEs/SDRs manually change the tier assigned by the system?

Threshold: Above 25% of touched leads means scoring weights need recalibration.

Action: Analyze which direction overrides go (up vs down). Adjust the dimension that's most frequently wrong.

Scope Acknowledgment

This system is a working demonstration framework, not a production-validated engine. The scoring logic, AI prompts, and routing rules are architecturally sound but have not been calibrated against real conversion data. In production, the first 4-6 weeks would be spent collecting AE feedback and adjusting weights based on actual meeting-booked and deal-closed outcomes. The framework is designed for that iteration cycle — the architecture supports weight tuning, A/B testing of scoring rules, and feedback-loop integration without structural changes.
